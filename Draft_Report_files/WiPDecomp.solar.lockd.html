Decomposition of Solar-Locked Ocean SST Anomalies

Executive Summary

This study decomposes global ocean sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies (1850–2026) to isolate secular anthropogenic warming from natural climate variability. Utilizing Singular Spectrum Analysis (SSA) paired with ESPRIT parameter estimation, primary oscillatory modes were characterized: the ~20.1-year bidecadal/Hale magnetic cycle (\(\vert{}z\vert{} \approx 1.0002\)), a split ~11-year Schwabe solar band (\(T_1 = 10.2\), \(T_2 = 12.3\) years) exhibiting amplitude modulation, and a sub-decadal ENSO recharge oscillator mode (\(3\text{--}6\) years).


To separate anthropogenic forcing without distorting low-frequency natural variability, a non-parametric Generalized Additive Model (GAM, \(k=30\), \(bs=\text{"cr"}\)) was anchored to the pre-1938 baseline. Subtracting this extrapolated baseline isolates a near-zero pre-WWII industrial component (\(0.0^\circ\text{C}\)) that accelerates sharply post-1950.


Modeling this industrial signal using an asymmetric Richards growth curve—with the secular growth rate fixed to the independent solar-locked trend (\(B \approx 0.05\))—yields a turning point at \(t_0 = 1980.5\). This represents an 8.2-year forward shift compared to a standard symmetric logistic fit (\(t_0 = 1988.7\)). Unconstraining the shape parameter (\(\nu \neq 1\)) accommodates early-stage upper-ocean thermal buffering while dampening non-physical asymptotic runaway post-2010.


Crucially, superimposing the isolated components reveals that multi-decadal natural troughs at ~1910 and ~1998 (\(\approx -0.35^\circ\text{C}\)) masked the true anthropogenic trajectory. The mid-to-late 20th-century “plateau” in unadjusted surface anomalies was not a deceleration in forced warming, but the destructive interference of a deep natural anomaly trough spanning 1995–2002 over a rapidly steepening industrial trend. Consequently, the true post-1960 anthropogenic impact is significantly larger than raw surface anomaly records suggest.

Methodology & Spectral Parameter Estimation

To objectively determine the fundamental periodicities, damping characteristics, and stationarity of the primary components isolated via Singular Spectrum Analysis (SSA), an ESPRIT (Estimation of Signal Parameters via Rotational Invariance Techniques) algorithm was applied to the leading reconstructed component pairs. The extracted complex roots (\(z = \vert{}z\vert{}e^{i 2\pi f}\)) yield both the characteristic oscillation frequencies (\(f\)) and their corresponding continuous growth/decay rates (\(\lambda = \ln \vert{}z\vert{}\)).

Parameter estimation reveals three distinct physical regimes within the ocean anomaly time series:

  1. Bidecadal / Hale Magnetic Mode (Cycle 1): Component Pair (1,2) isolates a dominant low-frequency cycle at \(T \approx 20.14\) years (\(241.7\) months). With a modulus \(\vert{}z\vert{} = 1.00018\) (\(\lambda = +0.00018\)), this mode exhibits near-perfect stationarity (\(\vert{}z\vert{} \approx 1\)), consistent with stable multi-decadal solar-magnetic forcing (the ~22-year Hale cycle) coupled with intrinsic bidecadal ocean variability.

  2. Split Schwabe Solar Band (Cycles 2 & 3): The ~11-year Schwabe solar band splits into twin sideband components at \(T_2 = 10.23\) years (\(122.8\) months) and \(T_3 = 12.32\) years (\(147.8\) months). This doublet structure indicates amplitude and phase modulation of the solar cycle across the observational record. Cycle 3 displays the strongest damping rate (\(\lambda = -0.00079, \vert{}z\vert{} = 0.99921\)), reflecting non-stationary energy exchange or decay in the lower sideband, whereas Cycle 2 (\(\lambda = +0.00057\)) reflects slight local amplitude expansion.

  3. Interannual Recharge Oscillator / ENSO Band (Cycles 4 & 5): The higher-frequency component pairs (4,5) and (9,10) resolve periods at \(T_4 = 5.25\) years (\(63.0\) months) and \(T_5 = 4.24\) years (\(50.9\) months), capturing the characteristic sub-decadal bandwidth of the equatorial Pacific recharge oscillator and interannual climate modes.

Decomposing the observed surface anomaly record into its pre-industrial baseline (pre-ind) and isolated anthropogenic forcing (indust) reveals a crucial phase interaction between natural variability and industrial emissions post-1960:

  1. Multidecadal Natural Troughs (~1910 and ~1998): The isolated pre-industrial baseline displays two major negative excursions reaching approximately \(-0.35^\circ\text{C}\): one centered near 1910 and a second prolonged trough between 1995 and 2002.

  2. Masking of Anthropogenic Acceleration: During the mid-to-late 20th century, the accelerating industrial forcing (eff.model, Olive) was partially offset by a strong negative phase in natural multi-decadal cycles. As a result, the unadjusted anomaly record (anoma, Red) exhibits a suppressed slope between 1960 and 2000.

  3. Implications for Climate Sensitivity: Isolating the natural baseline demonstrates that the true industrial contribution (indust, Cyan) accelerated more rapidly after 1960 than suggested by raw “hockey stick” surface projections. The apparent late-20th-century “plateau” or moderated slope in raw SSTs was not a slowdown in forced warming, but rather the destructive superposition of a deep natural anomaly trough upon an aggressively steepening anthropogenic trend.
